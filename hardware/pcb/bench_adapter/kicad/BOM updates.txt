*** CORRECTED 2026-08-10 — R5–R8 are 33 Ω, NOT 100 Ω. ***

My original note said 100 Ω here, reasoning that the flash resistors were "the
same fix as R1–R4" and that uniformity was worth having. That was wrong, and
bench_adapter_spec.md §2.1 (lines 85-88) already had the right answer:

  "J3's flash-PMOD resistors stay at 33 Ω: that link never crosses an
   independent power domain (flash chip has no board of its own to be
   'unpowered'), so it isn't exposed to this failure mode."

The 100 Ω value on J2 exists to cap fault current into an FPGA pin whose board
can be powered down while the Pico keeps driving — the damage that actually
happened on the Wukong J11. J3's flash is powered *by this adapter*, so there is
no unpowered-independent-domain scenario and nothing for the higher value to
protect against. "Keeps the board uniform" is not an engineering reason and it
would have silently overridden a documented, reasoned decision.

The _PRE_R net names confirm past-you intended resistors there.

R9/R10 are CS pull-ups, and they're well placed — on the header side of the series resistors, so the target's chip-select is held deasserted even while the Pico's GPIOs are hi-Z during reset and boot. That's the failure mode where a device gets spuriously selected during power-up. 10 k is conventional; anywhere 4.7 k–100 k works.

*** LED colours RESOLVED 2026-08-10. John's stock is modern InGaN, Vf ≈ 3.0 V,
    plus red available. ***

  R11 = 1 kΩ  — GREEN, off V5_IN (5 V). (5 − 3.0) / 1 kΩ ≈ 2 mA. A modern
                InGaN part at 2 mA is plenty bright for a power indicator and
                less glary on the bench than 3 mA. 680 Ω if you want it
                brighter; purely aesthetic.

  R12 = 330 Ω — **RED, and this is not interchangeable.** (3.3 − 1.9) / 330 Ω
                ≈ 4 mA.

**Do not fit a green/blue/white LED at R12.** At Vf ≈ 3.0 V off a 3.3 V GPIO
the headroom is 0.3 V before the RP2350's own output drop at 4 mA (another
0.1–0.3 V) is counted, and part-to-part Vf spread is ±0.2 V — comparable to the
entire budget. The result is not "dim but working", it is unpredictable: one
part faintly visible, the next dark, and the resistor value barely influencing
either. Red and amber are AlGaInP and stayed at Vf ≈ 1.9–2.1 V regardless of
how modern the part is, which is why red is specified here.

Rejected alternative, recorded so it is not re-proposed: wiring the LED between
5 V and GP14 so the GPIO sinks it. It appears to work — with Vf 3.0 the pin is
never pulled up, since 5 − 3.3 = 1.7 V is below the forward drop — but it puts a
5 V rail one component failure away from a non-5-V-tolerant pin. Same class of
fault as the J11 backfeed damage; not worth saving a part.

Also: RP2350 GPIO drive defaults to 4 mA (settable 2/4/8/12) — don't size for
20 mA like a 5 V part.
