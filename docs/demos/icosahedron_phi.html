What the classification means

The unlabelled icosahedron has 60 proper rotations and 60 orientation-reversing symmetries. The latter make its mirror superposable by a proper rotation, so the unlabelled solid is achiral. Distinguishing one vertex or assigning unequal vertex weights restricts the allowed group to the maps that preserve those values. Unique labels leave only the identity and make this labelled structure chiral relative to those labels. The vertex coordinates do not change between these cases.

In the single-weight view, v00 has exact weight 2 and each other vertex has weight 1. Its gold outline and w2 label persist while you select vertices or symmetries.

The determinant sign describes orientation of a three-dimensional linear map: +1 preserves orientation; −1 reverses it. It is not a label or a property of Galois conjugation.

Exact data and display

The 12 vertices are cyclic permutations of (0, ±1, ±φ), where φ=(1+√5)/2 and φ²=φ+1. Exact vertex permutations encode the classified maps; exact determinant signs separate proper and orientation-reversing operations. In the SVG, JavaScript approximates φ with Math.sqrt(5), then applies floating-point view rotation, perspective and screen placement.

Pixel positions, marker placement and face shading are display values. They are not exact geometric results and this page does not use the SPU GPU or claim hardware performance.
