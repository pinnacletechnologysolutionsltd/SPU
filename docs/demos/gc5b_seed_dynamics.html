What the measurements mean

  • Parity defect counts directed-edge coefficients that differ from the state transported through the one frozen reflection. It is a state comparison, not a direct measurement of law chirality.
  • Left-minus-right probe subtracts the two output-channel coefficients associated with directed edge (0,1). It is an exact linear observable in Q(√5), but only samples those two channels.
  • Coefficient sum is conserved by each permutation operator before weighting. Therefore the sum is multiplied by a+b per update: by 2 for the equal law and 4 for the signed law. The dense seed starts with sum zero, so its sum stays zero.
  • Maximum coefficient bits records the largest numerator/denominator bit length in the exact rational coefficients. It describes this finite run, not a long-term bound or hardware fit.
  • State hash fingerprints the complete canonical state. No repeat was found among steps 0–12; that says nothing about later recurrence or aperiodicity.

What follows from linearity: for fixed weights, the update is F = aTL + bTR. It distributes over sums of states and scales by field coefficients. So seed-dependent trajectories arise from applying the same operator to different initial vectors. The parity-defect count is nonlinear and depends on the chosen reflection; a nonzero value alone does not show that the law broke symmetry. The uniform seed’s zero defect is the negative control.

These are exact software results for four seeds, two profiles, one frozen reflection and 12 updates. The plots approximate coefficient magnitudes for display. No physical chirality, long-term dynamics, or hardware advantage is established.
