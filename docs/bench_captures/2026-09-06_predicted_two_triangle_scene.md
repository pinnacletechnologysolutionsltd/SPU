## What to look for, in order of diagnostic value

1. **A vertical colour boundary at x = 320** — the exact horizontal centre of
   the screen — running from the top edge down to where the two triangles
   separate at y ≈ 268. **Nothing in the geometry puts an edge there.** It
   exists only because `spu_depth_compare` is choosing the nearer surface per
   pixel. A boundary anywhere else, or a ragged one, is a depth fault rather
   than a coverage fault. This is the whole point of the spin.

2. **The boundary must reach the very top scanline.** Both triangles cover
   y = 0 from x = 40 to x = 600. The depth-anchor defect fixed on 2026-09-06
   was row-0-only, so a notch, a colour flip, or a displaced boundary on the
   top line specifically is the signature of that class of fault returning.

3. **Below y ≈ 268** the triangles no longer overlap: plain red on the left,
   plain green on the right, with black between them widening downward.

4. **Stair-stepping on the slanted edges is correct and expected.** There is
   no antialiasing. A *straight* horizontal top edge is also expected — it has
   A = 0.

Both share the top edge, so both cover scanline 0. tri0 is near on the left
and far on the right; tri1 the reverse. They interpolate 400..3600 across the
top edge in opposite directions, so they are equidistant at

```
400 + (x-40)*(3200/560) = 3600 - (x-40)*(3200/560)   =>   x = 320
```

The boundary is vertical because both depth planes share the same y gradient,
so their difference has no y term. That also means this scene is sensitive to
an x-direction depth error and **not** to a y-direction one — a deliberate
trade for a prediction that can be checked by eye on a handheld photo.
